Research Plan v2 · Ovarian Cancer Genomics

Knowledge, popularity, or circularity?

Does published ovarian cancer biology predict patient survival better than chance, better than gene popularity, and after removing knowledge that was itself learned from the test cohort? We answer this inside TCGA-OV alone, with no external validation cohort.

01

Why the plan changed

Starting from a seed set of 65 good-prognosis genes, we traced documented gene–gene relationships into Tier 1 and Tier 2. The literature turned out to report few explicit relationships but 2,175 actionable findings. Version 1 asked which of three gene panels predicts survival best. That design has two problems.

Random genes already predict survival. In breast cancer, 60% of published signatures did no better than random gene sets of the same size (Venet 2011). In late-stage ovarian cancer, only 8 of 14 published models beat random signatures, and the best reached a validation C-index of only about 0.56 to 0.60 (Waldron 2014). A ranking of three panels with no null distribution cannot show that any of them carries real biology.

The literature may have already seen the test set. Hundreds of ovarian signature papers were built on TCGA-OV. When genes from those papers are scored on TCGA-OV, the result is partly circular, and an external cohort would not have fixed that. Version 2 makes this circularity something we measure, and uses it as the validation axis that replaces external data.

03

Central hypothesis & five sub-hypotheses

Central hypothesis. Literature-curated genes carry prognostic signal in ovarian cancer beyond chance only after three artifacts are removed: the baseline prognostic signal that random genes show in TCGA-OV, gene popularity and the expression properties tied to it, and knowledge that was derived from TCGA-OV itself. What remains should come from pre-TCGA, cohort-independent literature, and should be strongest close to the seed genes in the network.

H1

Matched-null calibrationcore

Every panel is scored against 1,000+ gene sets matched on size, mean expression, variance, co-expression connectivity, and publication count. A panel counts as informative only if it beats this matched null.

New: earlier work used uniformly random genes. The matched null asks whether it matters which genes the literature picked, beyond what kind of genes they are.

H2

Popularity confound

The frequency panel’s apparent signal is explained by popularity-linked properties: proliferation (meta-PCNA), immune and stromal content (ESTIMATE), and expression variance. Tested with popularity-matched nulls and by adjusting for those axes.

New: the attention-bias finding has not been tested as a confound of prognostic gene panels.

H3

Provenance-held-out literaturecore

Our existing LLM pipeline tags each article with its publication date and whether it used TCGA data. Each literature panel is then rebuilt three ways: pre-2011 articles (before the TCGA-OV landmark paper), post-2011 TCGA-independent articles, and TCGA-derived articles. We predict TCGA-derived panels look strongest and lose their advantage over matched nulls once circularity is accounted for. The pre-TCGA panel is an out-of-sample test of prior knowledge.

New: validation moves from “new patients” to “knowledge the cohort could not have shaped.” This is how the study works without an external cohort.

H4

Knowledge-distance gradient

Signal above the matched null falls from seed to Tier 1 to Tier 2. A second test uses our own sparsity observation: do genes that appear in documented relationships carry more signal than genes that only appear inside findings?

New: turns the relationship-versus-finding gap into a dose-response test over network distance.

H5

Incremental value

For any panel that survives H1–H4, we measure the C-index gain over a clinical model (age, stage, residual tumor) plus known molecular axes (TCGA molecular subtypes, immune, proliferation). We expect most of the gain to disappear; the remainder is the clinically honest number.

New: reports the added value of literature knowledge, not its raw association with survival.

04

The design, drawn out

Each gene panel is compared against its own matched null on identical folds. The quantity we report is the gap between the panel and the null, not the panel’s raw score.

Each panel is judged by how far it beats its own matched null, on three outcomes.

05

Gene panels

The three panels from v1 stay, each refined so it can answer one hypothesis. Two reference arms are added.

01

Literature-structured

By tier and by source

Seed, Tier 1 and Tier 2 from the impact network (H4), each also split by article provenance (H3). The 65-gene seed list was provided by a collaborator; its origin is being traced, because a seed derived from TCGA-OV would make every tier circular.

02

Data-driven clustering

Fold-internal co-expression

Co-expression modules learned only on each training fold, so the arm never sees test patients. An externally supplied clustering set can be added as an extra arm once we confirm which data it was derived from.

03

Popularity

Corpus prominence

The most-mentioned genes. Counts are taken from an unrestricted gene extraction, because the current findings extractor keeps only seed-list genes and would make the arm overlap with the seed panel.

04

Reference arms

Floor and ceiling

Floor: property-matched nulls, plus non-cancer gene sets as negative controls. Ceiling: OTTA-SPOT (101 genes), the strongest published HGSOC expression signature.

07

Validation without external data

We replace the external cohort with checks that can each fail independently, all inside TCGA-OV.

  • 1Leave-one-site-out CV. TCGA-OV samples come from many tissue source sites. Holding out each site in turn is internal–external cross-validation, the closest thing to a new hospital.
  • 2Cross-platform transfer. Many TCGA-OV patients were profiled on Affymetrix U133A, Agilent 244K and RNA-seq. Train on one platform, test on another with disjoint patients.
  • 3Temporal holdout. Train on earlier diagnosis years, test on later ones.
  • 4Knowledge-side holdout (H3). Panels built only from literature that predates or does not use TCGA-OV.
  • 5Protein-level check. For panel genes on the RPPA array, test whether protein levels show the same direction of effect.
  • 6Negative controls. Non-cancer gene sets and permuted outcomes must sit at the null.
  • 7Leakage hygiene. Nested, repeated CV. All gene selection, clustering and normalization happen inside training folds.
08

What we report

Time-to-event · OS, PFI

  • Uno’s C-index and time-dependent AUC at 3 and 5 years
  • ΔC over matched null: panel minus null median, with empirical percentile
  • ΔC over clinical + molecular model (H5)

Binary · platinum resistance

  • AUROC, AUPRC, F1
  • Calibration slope and intercept
  • Same Δ-over-null framing as above

Fair comparison. Effects will be small (expected C around 0.55–0.62). Every comparison is paired on identical folds, panel size is either matched or swept, and the analysis is pre-registered before results are seen.

09

Roadmap

  1. Trace the seed list. Confirm where the 65 genes came from and whether TCGA-OV was involved.
  2. Tag the corpus. Extend the LLM extractor to record publication year, cohorts used (TCGA or not), and all genes mentioned, not only seed genes.
  3. Lock the cohort. TCGA-OV expression on each platform joined to TCGA-CDR outcomes and platinum status; fix sample counts.
  4. Build the matched-null generator and the negative-control sets.
  5. Pre-register hypotheses, metrics and thresholds.
  6. Run H1–H5 under the validation checks in section 07.
